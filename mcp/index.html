Open Distribution Intelligence System

Building an open superintelligence for everyone

OPDIS aims to bring together GPU time that people around the world choose to share. The goal is one persistent intelligence that can improve its own tools, carry a mission forward as computers come and go, and make its discoveries freely available to anyone.

One intelligence, many computers

Imagine volunteers using their home computers to investigate nerve repair. When one person shuts down for the night, another machine could pick up the same question with the studies, contradictions and unfinished work still intact. Someone with only a phone could use the findings.

If OPDIS found a flaw in how it compared studies, it could test a repair and keep it for the next investigation. Eventually it could improve the very tools it uses to find and test repairs. That is what recursive self-improvement means for this project.

What OPDIS is for

The ambition is to build the world’s most capable and widely accessible open superintelligence. Communities could use it to advance research on disease and disability and to challenge oppression with evidence they can inspect themselves. A longer-term goal is to automate work so earning a living no longer dictates how people spend their lives. Volunteers would choose which missions to support and how much computing time to give.

Where the prototype stands

Today, the coordinator can divide prompts among trusted workers and compare two sets of instructions. Two simulated workers have shared a batch. Persistent missions, verified self-repair and an open volunteer network are engineering goals.
